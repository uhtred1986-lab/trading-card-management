# #609 replacement and projection boundary proof

The independent `scripts/verify/battle-cleanup-replacement-gap-609.ts` uses synthetic generic 8-5-6 wording; the repository has no printed card with that wording. The rule order follows DBS manual 8-5-5 through 8-5-10-1. Movement-subject information follows 9-6-9-1-3 for ordinary same-player open-area moves, subject to the 9-6-9-1-2 exceptions. The proof runs with `DATABASE_URL=''` and no catalog or live database.

On the `6ee3bf5` main baseline, [Ultimate] changing a pre-announced Combo subject from Drop to Removed interrupts under #609 before 8-5-7. A legal 8-5-7 Auto choice could install a timed mandatory Warp replacement on that subject, but native 8-5-8 ignored it, moved the subject to Drop, and resolved the battle-end Auto. A timed optional replacement was discoverable by `replacementChoices` but the native mover never offered its `replaceMove` prompt. The difference came from `leaveRoute`: an [Ultimate] hook reads a held Combo departure, while ordinary automatic replacement discovery is scoped to `inPlayZones`, which excludes Combo. PR #691's old changed-destination fixture changed a saved card ID; the added proof exercises an Auto choice and persisted effect instead.

The bounded follow-up retains the existing 8-5-6/7/8/9/10 cleanup stages and adds a saved per-subject 8-5-8 `awaitingDeparture` choice. A native Combo departure with no qualifying generic watcher now offers optional and competing replacement routes to the affected player, freezes the offer through JSON reload, and consumes one answer before advancing its departure cursor. Declining leaves the Drop route; choosing a redirect records one physical move to its selected area. Every substitute runs through a persisted interpreter frame with the timed effect's bound variables; a question in that substitute pauses without replaying the departure. Wrong-actor and invalid-index answers leave the saved offer immutable. Two consecutive Combo subjects each receive their own offer and physical-move cursor. `vm/replace.ts` honors an explicit answer only when the native 8-5-8 event key is supplied; unrelated Combo moves and global automatic replacement discovery are unchanged.

The substitute's `substituteDeparture` record captures the actual movement of its replaced subject. Only while that saved frame runs does `vm/host.ts` tag its own Combo-to-Drop move as cleanup; its other moves keep ordinary timing. The runner re-enters the native 8-5-8 cursor before another checkpoint can drain movement Autos. An actual hand-origin Combo subject sent to Drop by a questioning substitute therefore joins the 8-5-9 special group with the battle-end Auto, and both resolve once in the player's selected 8-5-10 order. A substitute that leaves the subject in Combo supplies no special occurrence. The proof checks the physical move, saved prompt, pending group and event order, plus a bound-variable substitute that moves its selected hand card exactly once.

For a subject whose generic 8-5-6 Auto was already announced, a replacement with an unresolved route still raises typed #609. A pre-existing [Ultimate] or replacement choice interrupts before projected Drop information is invented. If an 8-5-7 Auto installs a different route, its own effect resolves after a JSON reload, but the cleanup interrupts before moving the subject or announcing the 8-5-9 battle-end group. A no-card answer to that Auto still completes the ordinary order: generic effect, physical Combo-to-Drop departure, then battle-end effect. The generic Auto activates once, before its choice is answered.

This does not settle what information a pre-announced Auto should receive when its eventual movement is substituted or redirected, nor whether the announced occurrence should remain pending. No printed-card or owner ruling in the repository establishes that combination. #609 remains open at that boundary; the proof claims no catalog/deck fuzz or new interpretation of the existing non-Battle Extra cleanup fixture.
