## Delivered

`src/lib/arena/policy/` is the provider-independent policy boundary. A policy is a pure function
`(PolicyInput, PolicyRng) → { answer, rng }` (`contract.ts`'s `Policy`); `uniformPolicy` is the
strength-free reference that proves the contract end to end.

- `observation.ts` — `PolicyObservation`, built from the seat's own masked `Snapshot` (the board
  `browser.ts`'s `projectSnapshot` draws), restricted further: own hand and own decklist counts,
  both public boards, counts of hidden zones, the open battle, the beat history, and the seat's
  own prompt (another seat's prompt is only its kind and actor). Instance ids of cards the seat
  cannot read are replaced by positional aliases (`?p2:hand:3`) — an id is a decklist position,
  so it could name a hidden card — and by `?` in beats, which also lose the card of any move the
  seat could not watch (a draw, a search, a life card into the other hand). No art, spend, taps,
  seed, RNG state or reveal epoch.
- `contract.ts` — `PolicyDecisions`: every engine legal move as a **menu** decision (stable id from
  the aliased move, sorted, so the same position gives the same ids however hidden cards lie), and
  for a `chooseCards` question one **compound `pick`** (`candidates`, `upTo`, `remaining`,
  `cover`) answered with a set and played as the rules engine's one-card answers. The session keeps
  the `DecisionKey`; `resolveAnswer` refuses `malformed`, `foreign` (another seat, or a seat not
  asked), `stale` (`at` is a fingerprint of seat, move count, observation and decisions) and
  `unknown`/`invalid` answers. Every engine action type and prompt kind is classified at compile
  time; `refereeRuling` (a program, Claude's job online) and `concede` (a button) are the two not
  offered.
- `rng.ts` — `PolicyRng`, `{ seed, n }`, SplitMix32 of the pair: serialisable, pure, never the
  game's RNG, never `Math.random` or a clock.
- `boundary.ts` — the only module that reads a `PinnedMatch`: `policyTurn(match, seat, revision)`
  and `applyDecision(match, key, answer)` (all or nothing; an engine refusal is `refused`).

Proof: `scripts/verify/arena-policy-contract.mts` (in `npm test`, before `ai-agent-sdk.ts`),
fixture `scripts/verify/arena-policy-fixture.ts` (synthetic cards that reach "choose up to 2" from
hand, Drop and the other Battle Area, and "choose one—"). Import guards: the policy layer's graph is
exactly its three modules (`policyLayerRule`), its source names no engine state, seed or ambient
randomness (`policyLayerSourceViolations`), each shown catching a sabotaged import; `boundary.ts` is
an entry of `arena-browser-boundary.mts`. At all 420 positions of #641's game, #642's scenarios
(via a new `observe` hook on `runScenario`) and two uniform-policy games, for both seats: no hidden
id or forbidden key in the input; permuted hidden identities, order and instance ids give identical
inputs and the same answer for the same policy RNG (with a negative control that sees the
permutation when the alias table is skipped); every legal move is exactly one decision that plays
identically to `applyMove`; compound picks (including multi-card ones) play as the engine's
one-card answers; stale, foreign and malformed answers are refused; and a policy-played game
replays from its moves alone, so the game's RNG was never drawn on.

Not changed: the online Claude opponent (`ai/`), the engine, `types.ts`, `ai/table.ts`,
`vm/view.ts` and `snapshot.ts` (the plan's suggested start points; nothing in them needed to move).
Not exercised on a real engine prompt: `optionalCost`, `offering`, `empowerCarry`, `orderPending`,
`counter`, `replaceMove` (the fixtures do not reach them; their legal moves are menu decisions like
every other). Instance-id aliasing is the policy's; the online board still names ids as before.
