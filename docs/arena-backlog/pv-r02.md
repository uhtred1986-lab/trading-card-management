**Rule-grounded behavior (#549).** Manual 9-6-4 permits paying or declining any necessary
Auto skill cost, including printed energy and action costs. A condition that is already true
cannot itself be declined (9-6-4-1-1); public-area cost-free/condition-only Autos retain compulsory
resolution. Unaffordable prices cancel activation under 9-6-3-2 and 5-8-2. The cost must
complete before the effect (5-8-2-2), and chosen X applies through resolution (20-5).
The private stored continuation retains source, skill index, master, trigger, subject,
cost targets, X and the paid cursor across generic option/card prompts and JSON reload.
A legal optional replacement remains its affected owner’s choice (9-10-2). Declining it can
complete payment; applying a different destination preserves the attempted payment and
replacement, cancels the Auto without resolving its effect, and resumes the pending queue
(5-8-2-2-1-1, 5-8-4). Effect-only replacement causes do not match cost moves.

**Affordability-limit correction and narrow search proof.** The planner's `NotYet` inherits `IllegalAction`; the former catch treated its exhausted search as an ordinary failed branch and could cancel a payable Auto as unaffordable. That catch now preserves the typed interruption. The registered `auto-costs-549.ts` case uses compiler-derived synthetic supported price grammar: rest one Battle Card, discard three of a 20-card hand, then rest the source. Choosing the source first is proven unable to pay the final self-rest only when the remaining instructions are exactly an acting-side hand choice, a move of those chosen cards to Drop, and a pure self-rest, with no saved source alias or possible cost-move replacement. The planner skips that dead branch and finds the later payable choice; the test checks its payment menu, decline, reload, cost-before-skill order, draw and opponent privacy. Later review extends this proof to saved source aliases and either decision actor. Mode resets, different destinations and possible replacements retain interpreter replay. The direct named transfer originally exhausted the search; the later proof below resolves that reviewed case. Other search shapes remain bounded and #549 stays open. No printed catalog card is asserted.

**Direct-transfer continuation proof.** The later compiler-derived hand-to-Drop then named Drop-to-Warp price now has a capacity proof before the search branches: cards already in Drop, saved picks still in hand, and at most the remaining hand-choice slots can provide its named Drop choice. When that sum is too small, the branch is genuinely unaffordable; when it is large enough, the planner tries matching cards first and still replays the whole payment in an isolated VM. The proof applies to a direct hand choice and move followed by a named Drop choice and Warp move, with either decision actor. Live effects, possible replacements and order-sensitive selectors retain interpreter replay. All-area Permanents disable negative capacity inference and use hint-only ordering before complete replay. Failed exact-state continuations are memoized without identifying different chosen orders. Root's final full test run measured a 30-card positive at about 358 ms, a negative at 3.6 ms and the different-chooser scenarios at 14.4 ms; timings depend on the host. Saved source aliases with the reviewed impossible final self-rest are proved false in under a millisecond. Dormant replacement and other unsupported search shapes retain typed unknown rather than false. The synthetic fixture does not establish a printed card with this exact chain.

**Additional complete price branches.** A `moveTo under` payment now requires one existing host and a successful move into that host's under-pile (23-2); a missing host cancels as unpayable before skill activation. A deck/life `shuffle` may follow another payable price step only as the final instruction on every branch, so no later price decision can depend on randomized order. A completed compulsory nonempty choice and strict move/mode/concealment transition establishes the paid prefix; replay still verifies the chosen cards, destination and any replacement. Previously admitted top-level terminal shuffles after declared dynamic or zero-X prices retain their existing grammar; the stronger paid-prefix proof applies only to the newly admitted conditional and nested adjunct. Bare conditional free branches, optional empty choices before a new conditional adjunct, standalone, inspected-only and nonterminal shuffles remain typed unknown. Fixed scenarios check pay, decline, reload, a false conditional branch, impossible hand count, replacement diversion, transition/modal preservation and event order before skill activation. **Interchangeable-candidate search (13 Oct 2026).** The generic affordability search now replays only the first of each class of interchangeable candidates at a card prompt. Two candidates share a class when they stand in the same zone of one side, their card instances are identical apart from their id, and nothing else in the state (saved variables, effects, pending records, the lone answer, an id inside an occurrence key) names either. Renaming one to the other is then a symmetry of the state, and the interpreter treats ids as opaque, so the two answers differ only in those cards' positions within the zone. The reduction therefore applies only where position is unobservable: never in Deck or Life (read from the top), never in an area that a positional selector (`take`/`fromEnd`) in any program of a card in the game, a keyword body, an effect or the remaining price reads, and never in an area a remaining non-choice price instruction resolves in bulk. `scripts/verify/auto-search-549.ts` compares the reduced search with the exhaustive one on 72 small boards (four price shapes, three interleaved hands, both source modes, no/dormant/aimed replacement) and finds them equal, with both payable and unpayable outcomes; it checks each guard and each reference that keeps a copy apart. The audit's P0 board (dormant conditional replacement, rested source, 20-card hand) is now proven unpayable in about 10 ms instead of interrupting, an active source on the same board pays by declining the replacement, and twenty distinct cards still exceed the bound and stay typed unknown. End to end, the compiled synthetic price is cancelled as unaffordable without an interruption on the blocked board, and on the open board pays once through a declined replacement prompt before the skill fires and draws. `auto-costs-549.ts` now asserts that its two former unknown replacement boards are decided false with the reduction and still unknown without it. Aegis shares `canFinishAutomaticPrice`, so the same reduction decides some Aegis prices it formerly left unknown; its admitted grammar is unchanged. The bound is not removed: distinct-card searches beyond 2,000 visited states remain typed unknown.

**KO and add-marker prices (13 Oct 2026).** The public deckplanet catalog feed answered this time (it now caps `limit` at 500, so the read used 14 pages; `src/lib/catalog/deckplanet.ts` still asks for `limit=100000` and gets an error object back — reported, not changed here). Compiling every catalogued [Auto] and [Aegis] price with `compileCostProgram` and running `readableAutomaticPrice` over it (scratch script, no database): 2,615 catalogued Auto prints (variants included) carry a printed cost and 654 of those compile to an action price; 630 were already admitted, and 650 are after this change. Of the 24 refused before, 17 are KO (`choose → ko` on own cards, e.g. BT25-075 "Choose 1 of your Battle Cards without [Unique] and KO it", BT29-068 "Choose 1 of your <Vegeta> cards and KO it", BT18-021 "other ≪Shadow Dragon≫"; and `ko(self)`, e.g. DB2-172 "[Auto] KO this card: When one of your opponent's Battle Cards … attacks, negate the attack"), 3 are BT31's "Add 1 marker to this card", 2 are BT12-023's face-down flip, one `comboFrom` (BT29-030) and one `power` (DB2-011). **As compiled, no printed Auto or Aegis action price is a draw, a look, addLife, lifeDownTo, a play or a flip to the back**, and no catalogued [Aegis] line carries an action price at all. The four prints still refused are BT12-023 and its PR print (a conditional face-down flip outside the verified Life choice), BT29-030 (`comboFrom`) and DB2-011 (a self power loss). So this increment admits the two printed families. `readableAutomaticPrice` accepts `ko` only as "KO this card" or immediately after a compulsory choice (count ≥ 1, not up-to, no `atMost`, chooser you) of your own cards in the Battle Area or in play, and `addMarker` only on this card with a printed positive count; every other KO/marker shape still falls to the generic typed unknown. The strict host offers only own Battle-Area cards that can be KO'd now (5-12-1: never the Leader that "your <Vegeta> cards" also reads, nor an opponent's card, an [Indestructible] card of another master, or one under a "can't be KO'd" prohibition), refuses a KO the interpreter would skip, and counts the KO paid only when the card is in its owner's Drop afterwards (5-8-2-2-1-1). Judgement call: a KO paid as a cost is still a KO (5-12-2: the instruction says "KO"), so a KO replacement answers it and "when this card is KO'd" Autos pend; a replacement of removal "by a skill" (an effect) does not. No local FAQ exists and a web search of the official Q&A pages found no ruling on a KO paid as a cost; the owner may want to confirm this reading. A KO replacement applied by the card's master that keeps the card or sends it elsewhere fails the attempted payment and cancels the Auto (5-8-4); a mandatory one makes the price unaffordable before it is offered. An added marker must be on the card afterwards, and the card must be in play. `scripts/verify/ko-marker-price-549.ts` asserts, with fixed expectations: the catalog wordings compile to the admitted shapes and eight unproved shapes stay unknown under both owners; a real play offers a "KO this card" price, decline pays nothing, payment KOs before the skill and the source's own KO'd Auto then draws; a source outside the Battle Area or under a "can't be KO'd" Permanent is unaffordable with no mutation; the own-card choice excludes Leader, opponent, prohibited and hand cards but admits a card protected only from the opponent's skills; wrong-actor, ineligible, empty and stale answers are refused byte-for-byte; reload mid-price resolves identically and charges once; optional/mandatory/effect-only KO replacements; marker pay/decline/unaffordable; both viewer projections (opponent waits with no legal move, no cost cursor in either view). Mutation runs confirmed the replacement-reason, arrival and target checks are each load-bearing. The legacy engine's own planner (`engine/state.ts` `canPayCostProgram`) is frozen and unchanged; this is rules-engine behaviour. The owner confirmed the judgement call on 13 Oct 2026: a KO paid as a cost is still a KO (5-12-2).

**The last printed prices (13 Oct 2026).** A second read-only pass over the public catalog feed (14 pages of 500, `src/lib/catalog/deckplanet-fetch.ts`; shaped with `shapeCatalog`, compiled with `rulesFromCompiler`, no database) reproduced the #708 baseline exactly with `origin/main`'s validator — the only refused Auto action prices were BT12-023 (both prints), BT29-030 and DB2-011 — and now finds **every** catalogued Auto action price readable by the strict host: 447 of 447 distinct cards (677 of 677 counting the feed's variant rows; #708 counted prints differently and reported 654). No catalogued [Aegis] line carries an action price. The three shapes, from the catalog's own text:
- **BT12-023** “If your Leader Card is a red <King Piccolo> card and you flip 1 face-up card in your life face down:” compiles to the Leader condition plus `choose(life, count 1, filter faceUp) → faceUp(false)`. The verified own-Life face-turn pair (#695) now also admits a selector filter that asks only for the face the turn leaves; any other filter, an up-to pick or a contradictory face-up filter on a face-up turn stays typed unknown. The host offers only face-up Life cards and verifies the actual turn (5-8-2-2); the Leader condition stays a compulsory condition (9-6-4-1-1), so without it nothing is offered.
- **BT29-030** “Use 1 card from under this card in a combo with its skills negated for the turn:” compiles to `choose(under, count 1) → comboFrom(negated)`. Admitted only as a compulsory single own pick from the source's pile immediately used in the combo. The interpreter's `comboFrom` silently skips a card when no battle has the master in it or when it is already in a Combo Area; the strict host offers only cards under the source that can be used in a combo now — a Battle Card with a combo cost and combo power (5-7-2, 5-7-2-1), in a battle the master is in (5-7-1), with no combo prohibition — and verifies arrival in the Combo Area (5-8-2-2-1-1) and the standing negation (3-1-4-3 covers exactly this wording). The combo is performed without its combo cost (5-7-3-1). Judgement call: like a cost KO, a combo paid as a cost keeps its `combo` move cause, which `rulesets/dbs/ops.rules` declares for `comboFrom`; no printed watcher reads that cause for a card leaving an under-pile, so the test cannot observe it.
- **DB2-011** “This card gets -15000 power for the turn:” compiles to `power(self, -15000, turn)`. Admitted only as a printed negative integer on the source for the turn or the battle. Power may go below zero (2-7-2), so the change is always performable on a card whose power the game reads; the host requires the source in the Battle or Leader Area and verifies the effect is in force. Judgement call: a source that has left play before its checkpoint cannot pay — an effect on a card outside play changes nothing the game reads, and the effect would not follow it back (3-1-4); the manual has no explicit sentence for a power cost, and no official ruling was found locally.

**The 2,000-visit bound, re-measured (13 Oct 2026).** Printed Auto price shapes are almost all one pick then one instruction; the only printed multi-step shapes are pairs of single picks (e.g. `choose(hand:1) → moveTo(deck) → choose(under:1) → moveTo(hand)`), and the largest printed picks are three cards (`choose(battle:3) → moveTo(removed)`, `choose(hand:3)`, `choose(drop:3)`, `choose(energy:3)`, `choose(zDeck:3)`). A synthetic stress ran every catalogued Auto action price through `canFinishAutomaticPrice` with the source Active and Rested, on crowded boards of distinct real catalog cards (20- and 30-card hands, a 24-card Drop, vanilla and skilled fodder, a standing optional leave replacement): 6,704 decisions, **no typed unknown**, slowest 1.0 s. The bound is still reachable by a printed shape, but only on an unrealistic board: “Choose 3 of your Battle Cards and remove them from the game” with every one of 14 distinct Battle Cards under a mandatory leave replacement enumerates 14·13·12 ordered picks and interrupts (13 Battle Cards decide false). Latency is the sharper finding: the same diverted board takes about 0.8 s with 8 Battle Cards and 8.6 s with 12 before deciding false, because every leaf replays the whole price on a cloned state. `arena-deck-audit --check --play 20` (80 games between its two real decks) still stops nowhere. Removing the bound still needs a sound pruning for picks whose later move a mandatory replacement diverts; it is not attempted here.
