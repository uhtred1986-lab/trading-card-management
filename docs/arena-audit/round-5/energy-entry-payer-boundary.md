# R476 payer replacement boundary

The bounded optional-entry bridge must not suspend an original Energy movement for an unproven payer substitute. The continuation wrapper previously forwarded a child interrupt to the parent host but returned `suspended`; native flow could stop before running the queued substitute and expose a stale chooser. Earlier isolated host tests did not prove native scheduler behavior for that transfer.

The native bridge now refuses payer replacement routes before the first selected payer moves, checking both currently bound payer slots. An unselected Drop card with a replacement does not block the selected payment, and declining the optional effect does not inspect or move payers. Saved replacement continuations are rejected at validation and execution boundaries, including an already-completed child; unexpected interrupt transfers also refuse instead of queuing work behind a stale prompt. This is an explicit unsupported-route boundary, not implementation of substitute timing or replacement receipts.

`energy-entry-payer-boundary-5.ts` proves 44 native controls through saved `RULES.apply` actions across both seats. Questionful substitutes, inline substitutes and direct replacement routes on either payer are rejected before either payer or the incoming card moves. The result is an explicit interruption rather than a repeated chooser; replacement bodies do not execute. Decline and choices that avoid the affected payer still complete normally. Four malformed saved replacement markers per seat reject without movement. Two additional continuation controls prove a completed child cannot bypass the guard (`energy-entry-continuation-5.ts`: 246 checks).

The two ordinary payer movements remain ordinary effects, not costs. Existing partial movement and completion-receipt semantics remain covered by the focused effect/native suites. This patch makes no catalog/compiler admission, does not settle the incoming-Drop self-payer question, and does not add Charge support. No database write, migration, dependency change or deployment is included.
